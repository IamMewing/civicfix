Scope · Draft

CivicFix

An AI agent that turns a photo of a real civic problem into a researched, correctly-routed, ready-to-file complaint for the right Hyderabad authority.

The Unique Kernel

It doesn't explain or summarize a problem — it investigates the bureaucracy. Given a photo of a pothole, broken streetlight, or water leak, it researches — live, with cited sources — which specific authority is responsible, and produces a complaint that's actually ready to file. The shift: “I don't know who handles this” → “I researched it — here's who does, why, and where to file it.”

Who It's For

Hyderabad residents who commute daily and regularly encounter civic hazards — potholes especially. Today, most people in this position either don't report problems at all, because they don't know whether GHMC, TGSPDCL/TSSPDCL, or HMWSSB handles it, or give up navigating multiple confusing government portals.

They've personally hit potholes while traveling, and are aware that pothole-related accidents in the city have killed people. This isn't an abstract prompt.

The Core Loop

What “Working” Looks Like

Open CivicFix on a phone → take a real photo of a pothole → add a short description → within moments the issue is identified, the correct Hyderabad authority is named with its official source cited, and a complete complaint is drafted.

Tap “Open Official Reporting Channel” → land on the real government portal with everything ready to file → submit it for real.

The “oh, that's cool” beat: watching the agent go from “I don't know who handles this” to a correctly-sourced, ready-to-file complaint in seconds — then seeing that complaint actually filed on the real government site, on camera, in the demo video.

Scope Boundary

Now In the proof of concept
  • Hyderabad only. Three issue categories: potholes/road damage, broken streetlights, water leakage/drainage.
  • Photo capture + typed description. Browser geolocation for location. No native app.
  • Vision classification of the issue from the photo.
  • Grounded authority research — live search backed by pre-verified official sources for the 3 categories; cites what it used.
  • One complete, formatted complaint per report: issue, location, description, evidence, category, authority, sources.
  • Honest hand-off to the real official reporting channel. No fake submission, no fake status tracking.
  • Mobile-first responsive web app (PWA).
Later Worth doing, not now
  • Other cities beyond Hyderabad — architecture designed to extend, not implemented for v1.
  • Voice input via the browser's built-in speech recognition, once the typed-text core loop works end-to-end.
  • An “evidence sufficiency” check that flags an incomplete photo/description and asks for more.
  • A personal history/local tracker of problems reported.
Cut Explicitly out
  • Automated submission via government API. No public API exists; faking a “submitted” state would violate the hackathon's “must function as depicted” rule.
  • Live tracking of government response/resolution. Same reason — nothing to back it.
  • Citing specific municipal code/legal sections. Real hallucination risk. Replaced with “official process + cited source” instead.
  • Native Android/iOS app. Unneeded — browser geolocation and camera capture cover it, and a PWA ships far faster solo.
